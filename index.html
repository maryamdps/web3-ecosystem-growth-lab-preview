01 / THE PROJECT BRIEF

What is this strategy trying to solve?

Imagine an Ethereum Layer 2 network with the technology in place, but competing for attention in a crowded ecosystem. The challenge is not simply to explain that it is faster or cheaper. It is to give users, developers and partners a compelling reason to build on it and return to it.

The business problem

Many people hear about networks, transactions and low fees without understanding what they can actually do there. Technical advantages alone do not guarantee active users, useful applications or a thriving ecosystem.

The strategic question

Which audience and real-world use case offer the clearest starting point — and how should the ecosystem communicate, acquire and activate those users?

Scope: an illustrative ecosystem-level GTM strategy. It is not a strategy commissioned by a named Layer 2 company, and the recommendations would need validation against a real network's capabilities, data and market.

02 / THE TECHNOLOGY — IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Web3, blockchain and Layer 2 — how do they fit together?

Web3 is a broad term for internet services that use blockchain technology. A blockchain is a shared digital record that a network helps maintain and verify. Ethereum is one such network; a Layer 2 connects to an underlying blockchain like Ethereum to help applications handle activity with greater capacity and often lower costs. The four stages below show how the technology becomes something people can actually use.

01FOUNDATION◈

Ethereum

The underlying blockchain that provides the base environment and security foundations for the ecosystem.

Beginner example: Think of Ethereum as a shared public record book and rulebook. When someone sends a transaction or uses an app, computers on the network check that it follows the rules — for example, that the person is allowed to make the transfer — and update the shared record. This lets users and apps verify the same history instead of relying on one company to keep the only copy. Many Layer 2 networks use Ethereum to publish data or settle activity, although their security designs differ.

02SCALING↗

Layer 2 network

A network designed to make blockchain activity more scalable and practical for everyday applications.

Beginner example: Imagine a local express line connected to the main railway. It handles more journeys away from the busiest central tracks, then uses the main network in a defined way to report or settle activity. This can help apps handle more activity, often at a lower cost.

03UTILITY▣

Apps and services

Products built on the network — such as payment tools, games, marketplaces and finance applications.

Beginner example: Think of an app as the service passengers actually use. For example, a freelancer could use a payment app built on a Layer 2 network to receive a supported digital payment from an overseas client.

04ADOPTION◎

People using them

Growth happens when people discover an app, understand its value, complete a useful action and come back.

Beginner example: The freelancer discovers the app, checks that it supports their country and payment needs, receives a first payment successfully, and returns to use it for the next client. That repeat behaviour is adoption.

Why this matters for marketing

Do not lead with “our network uses rollups”. Lead with the useful experience the network helps make possible. Explain the technology only when it helps someone understand the benefit, trust the product or take the next step.

03 / CHOOSE A USE CASE

Start with a problem people already have.

A network can support many applications, but a broad message aimed at everyone is difficult to remember. This strategy uses cross-border payments as an illustrative entry point because the user problem is familiar and easy to communicate.

The user problem

A freelancer works with clients in different countries and wants to receive money with fewer surprises.

THE NEEDGet paidBy an overseas client
THE EXPERIENCESimple stepsClear fees and status
THE OUTCOMEAccess fundsWith a usable cash-out route
THE VALUECome backUse it for the next payment

Why this is a useful starting point

A clear job-to-be-done gives the ecosystem a more concrete story.

  • It starts with a recognisable need, not crypto terminology.
  • It creates a practical way to demonstrate the user experience.
  • It gives partners and developers a use case to build around.
  • It creates measurable actions beyond wallet connections.

Important: an L2 network does not automatically provide payment services, stablecoin liquidity or fiat cash-out. A real campaign must be built with applications and providers that support the relevant markets and journey.

04 / AUDIENCE & POSITIONING

Who needs to believe in the ecosystem — and why?

Ecosystem growth has more than one audience. Users need a useful product; developers need a reason to build; partners need confidence that the ecosystem can attract demand.

01END USERS↗

Freelancers & remote workers

Need a straightforward way to receive international payments through an application that supports their markets.

Message: Get paid globally, with a clearer payment experience.
02BUILDERS⌘

Payment app developers

Need users, distribution, useful infrastructure, documentation and ecosystem support.

Message: Build payment experiences for real user needs.
03ECOSYSTEM PARTNERS◇

Wallets, providers & communities

Can improve discovery, onboarding, access and trust when the product experience is ready.

Message: Help make the end-to-end journey work.
04PAYMENT ACCESS⇄

On- and off-ramp providers

Help users move between supported digital assets and local currency, making it possible to access or cash out funds where services are available.

Message: Connect the digital payment to a usable local outcome.

05 / THE USER JOURNEY

The journey from discovery to habit.

A practical framework for examining where people progress — and where they get stuck. Marketing can create awareness, but activation depends on a working application, clear onboarding and reliable support.

01 / DISCOVER

“What is this?”

Someone hears about a network or finds an app that appears relevant.

Possible friction: unclear audience or value proposition.

02 / UNDERSTAND

“Is this for me?”

They understand what it does, how to start, what it costs and what risks exist.

Possible friction: jargon, uncertainty or low trust.

03 / ACTIVATE

“I did it.”

They complete a meaningful first action and experience the product's value.

Possible friction: wallet setup, fees or confusing steps.

04 / RETURN

“I'll use this again.”

They find ongoing utility, community or a reason to return.

Possible friction: no repeat use case or weak product value.

What the ecosystem team can do

  • Recruit and support relevant application partners.
  • Coordinate co-marketing with wallets, apps and communities.
  • Make the ecosystem easier to discover and understand.
  • Share developer resources and user education.

What must be true before launch

  • A partner app supports the target user journey.
  • Fees, availability and supported markets are verified.
  • Onboarding and customer support are ready.
  • Claims about speed, cost and security are substantiated.

01SHORT VIDEO▶

Explain the problem

“What should you check before receiving an international payment?”

Purpose: Make a confusing topic feel relevant and easy to understand.

Expected outcome: More qualified attention and visits to the campaign or partner page.

02CAROUSEL▤

Make the journey clear

Show the steps from client payment to funds received, using a real partner app.

Purpose: Break the process into clear, manageable steps.

Expected outcome: Better understanding and fewer unanswered questions before trying the app.

03PARTNER CONTENT◎

Demonstrate the product

A freelancer or creator walks through the supported experience with transparent disclosures.

Purpose: Show a real, supported product journey through a relatable person.

Expected outcome: Greater trust and more users exploring the partner app.

04FOLLOW-UP↻

Answer objections

Address availability, fees, safety, network choice and support before asking people to try it.

Purpose: Resolve practical concerns that may stop someone from taking action.

Expected outcome: More confident first attempts and fewer avoidable drop-offs.
