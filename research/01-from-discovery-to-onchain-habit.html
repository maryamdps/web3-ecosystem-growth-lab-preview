01 / Start here

First, what are we actually talking about?

In one sentence

A blockchain is a shared digital record that many computers help maintain. Instead of one company keeping the only copy, the network collectively checks and records updates.

The bigger picture

A blockchain is not the whole business.

Think of it as the underlying infrastructure. The wallets, apps, developers, communities and services built around it make up the wider ecosystem — and shape whether people find it useful.

Think of a cityRoads, electricity and water are infrastructure. Shops, homes, services and people make the city useful.
↔
Think of a blockchain ecosystemThe network is infrastructure. Wallets, apps, developers and users create the experiences people come for.

The city comparison is a learning aid, not a literal technical description. Blockchains have different designs, trade-offs and governance models.

Picture the ecosystem

A blockchain is the shared foundation. The ecosystem is the network of people and products built around it.

Simplified concept map: real ecosystems contain many more participants, services and dependencies.

02 / The vocabulary

Seven terms, without the jargon.

▤

Blockchain

A shared record of transactions and data, maintained by a network of computers rather than one central database.

Think: a shared record book.
◉

Wallet

Software that manages the keys used to access digital assets and approve actions. It does not literally store coins inside the app.

Think: keys to your digital account.
⌘

Transaction

An instruction submitted to a blockchain — for example, sending a token or interacting with an application.

Think: submitting a request.
⚙

Smart contract

A program on a blockchain that runs when someone interacts with it, following rules written in its code.

Think: an automated rulebook.
▦

dApp

Short for decentralised application: a user-facing product that uses blockchain networks or smart contracts for some of its functions.

Think: an app with blockchain components.
↗

On-chain

An activity recorded or executed on a blockchain. Not every action in a crypto product happens on-chain.

Think: visible in the network record.
Ⅱ

Layer 1 / Layer 2

Layer 1 is a base blockchain. A Layer 2 is a separate system built to help scale a base network, with its own design and security trade-offs.

Think: base network + supporting system.

04 / Why growth is hard

Getting someone to hear about a network is only step one.

A person might see a campaign, download a wallet and still never use a blockchain app. Growth means helping them move from curiosity to a useful first experience — and giving them a reason to return.

The growth loop

A strong ecosystem does more than attract attention. It turns a useful first experience into a reason to return.

The loop is a proposed growth framework for analysis, not a measured funnel from any one network.
01 / DISCOVER

Find something relevant

They hear about a network or discover an app that appears useful.

Growth question: Can the right audience find it?
02 / UNDERSTAND

Feel confident enough

They understand what the product does, what it costs and what risks to consider.

Growth question: Is the value clear?
03 / ACTIVATE

Achieve a first success

They complete a meaningful action, such as making a payment or using an app.

Growth question: Can they get started?
04 / RETURN

Have a reason to come back

They find repeat value rather than completing one action and leaving.

Growth question: Is it useful again?

06 / What this means for business

Technology creates possibility. Growth makes it understandable and useful.

Insight 01 / Education

Explain the job, not just the technology.

People are more likely to understand a product when they can see what it helps them do — send money, trade, collect or build — before learning every technical term.

Insight 02 / Activation

Make the first useful action easier.

Wallet setup, network selection, fees and transaction approvals can create friction. Clear guidance and transparent risk explanations matter.

Insight 03 / Retention

Reward ongoing utility, not empty activity.

Campaigns can create attention, but long-term growth depends on whether products solve a recurring problem or offer lasting value.

07 / What I would test next

Three growth experiments — still hypotheses.

The following ideas translate the research into practical work. They have not been executed or measured; the next step is to test them through product walkthroughs and user feedback.

Experiment A / Discovery

Build journeys around real needs

Instead of a long directory of apps, organise discovery around what someone wants to do: make a payment, explore a game, trade or build a product.

Experiment B / Activation

Design a first-success guide

Explain the wallet, network, fee and approval steps in plain language, then help a user complete one low-risk, meaningful task.

Experiment C / Retention

Create a reason to return

Connect users to recurring utility, helpful product updates or community learning — not only short-term reward campaigns.

How success could be measured

Track progress, not vanity metrics alone.

Potential measures include discovery-to-app visits, onboarding completion, first successful action, time to first value, and repeat meaningful activity. Actual targets should be set only after a baseline is established.
