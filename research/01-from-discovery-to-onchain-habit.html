03 / Growth framework

The real challenge is not awareness. It's progression.

Across ecosystems, a useful growth lens is to follow a user from first exposure to repeated value. The stages below are an analytical framework for this project, not a claim that any chain currently performs well or poorly at each stage.

STAGE 01

Discover

Can the right user find a relevant app or use case?

STAGE 02

Understand

Can they understand the value, risks and steps required?

STAGE 03

Activate

Can they complete a meaningful first action successfully?

STAGE 04

Return

Is there a compelling reason to come back and build a habit?

Observed from public materials

Discovery and onboarding are increasingly product surfaces.

Ethereum provides app and wallet discovery paths; Solana makes developer learning and build paths explicit; Base brings app discovery into a consumer product; X Layer foregrounds bridge, explorer and developer entry points.

Research hypothesis

The hand-off between stages is where growth may leak.

A user can discover an ecosystem without understanding which app to trust, understand a product without completing a first transaction, or activate once without finding a repeat-use case. These are questions to test through product walkthroughs and user research.

04 / Initial opportunities

Three experiments worth designing.

These are proposed growth experiments derived from the desk research. They have not been run, and no outcomes or performance lifts are claimed.

Experiment A / Discovery

Use-case-led ecosystem routes

Build curated paths for distinct jobs-to-be-done — for example, stablecoin payments, earning, trading or building — rather than presenting a newcomer with an undifferentiated list of apps.

Measure: route click-through → app connection → first meaningful action.
Experiment B / Activation

First-success onboarding

Create a guided, low-risk first task with plain-language explanations of wallets, networks, fees and transaction approval. Make the successful outcome visible immediately.

Measure: onboarding completion → first successful transaction → time to completion.
Experiment C / Retention

Progress-based return loops

Give users a reason to return based on ongoing utility — portfolio actions, payment history, app updates or community progress — rather than relying only on short-lived rewards.

Measure: D7 / D30 return rate and repeat meaningful actions, segmented by use case.

Methodology & limitations

Research snapshot dated 17 November 2026. This is a qualitative review of publicly available official materials. It does not include chain-level analytics, independent user interviews, controlled usability testing, or audited retention data. Officially published metrics and strategic statements are treated as self-reported. Recommendations are hypotheses for future testing, not measured results.
