GTM STRATEGY 01 · PRODUCT GROWTH

Base App.
From onchain super-app to everyday utility.

A proposed go-to-market strategy to help more people discover a relevant use case, complete a first valuable action and build a reason to return — without relying on crypto-native jargon or incentive-led activity alone.

01 / EXECUTIVE STRATEGY

Do not market every feature to everyone.

Base App brings together trading, social features, payments, app discovery and ways to earn. The opportunity is to make this breadth feel useful rather than overwhelming: acquire users through a specific job-to-be-done, then introduce adjacent features after they experience value.

Strategic thesis

Position Base App as a simple entry point to useful onchain actions. Build separate acquisition journeys around distinct motivations — moving money, discovering markets, creating content or exploring apps — and connect them through a shared, low-friction onboarding and retention system.

02 / AUDIENCE PRIORITIES

Segment by motivation, not by crypto sophistication alone.

These are proposed priority segments for testing, not claims about Base's current user mix. Prioritisation balances clarity of user need, repeat-use potential and the ability to communicate value without promising financial returns.

PRIORITY 01 · EVERYDAY MONEY MOVERS

People who need to send or receive money.

Digitally confident people with cross-border payment needs, remote work income, online communities or international family and peer networks.

  • Entry use case: send / receive stablecoins.
  • Message: move money across borders with a clear, guided experience.
  • Barrier to validate: trust, cash-in/out, recipient readiness, fees and local availability.
  • Activation: complete a first successful transfer.
PRIORITY 02 · CREATOR-COMMUNITY PARTICIPANTS

Creators and their existing audiences.

Creators, niche community leaders and followers interested in new ways to publish, connect and participate in creator-led ecosystems.

  • Entry use case: publish, discover and engage with creator content.
  • Message: bring your community into an open onchain social experience.
  • Barrier to validate: unfamiliar social graph, earning mechanics and audience liquidity.
  • Activation: follow a creator, publish or complete a meaningful interaction.
PRIORITY 03 · ACTIVE MARKET EXPLORERS

People already comfortable with digital markets.

Existing crypto users seeking asset discovery, trading tools and a more social way to follow market activity.

  • Entry use case: discover an asset and understand its details before trading.
  • Message: explore markets with useful context in one place.
  • Barrier to validate: trust, asset quality, risk understanding and differentiation.
  • Activation: complete a considered first trade.
Recommendation: pilot one segment at a time in markets where the relevant product features, fiat rails, compliance requirements and support coverage are available. Do not assume every feature is available in every country.

04 / ACQUISITION STRATEGY

Build distribution around each use case.

Avoid a single generic “join Web3” campaign. Match the channel, creative and landing experience to the motivation that brought the user in.

Creator and community partnerships

Partner with credible creators in finance education, digital culture, creator tools and cross-border communities. Co-create task-led walkthroughs and disclose commercial relationships.

BEST FIT · CREATOR / SOCIAL DISCOVERY
Search-led education

Build plain-language explainers around real questions: how stablecoin transfers work, how to understand a wallet prompt, or how to evaluate an onchain asset.

BEST FIT · HIGH-INTENT DISCOVERY
Product-led referrals

Make it easy to invite a recipient, creator collaborator or friend into a specific shared action. Reward only where compliant and never make rewards the reason to transact.

BEST FIT · MONEY MOVERS / SOCIAL
Lifecycle and owned media

Use opt-in email, in-app education and relevant notifications to guide unfinished onboarding and introduce the next feature based on observed behaviour.

BEST FIT · ACTIVATION / RETURN
App and ecosystem distribution

Work with relevant app developers and ecosystem partners to create contextual entry points from the product or community where the use case already exists.

BEST FIT · APP DISCOVERY
Selective paid acquisition

Run small, controlled campaigns only after the segment-specific landing page and activation event are instrumented. Optimise to quality actions, not installs alone.

05 / FUNNEL & ACTIVATION

Make the first valuable action the centre of the journey.

01 / REACH
Relevant discovery

Creator content, search, referrals and partner distribution show a specific job-to-be-done.

Measure: qualified visits
02 / UNDERSTAND
Clear expectations

Segment landing page explains what the app does, availability, requirements and risks.

Measure: onboarding starts
03 / SET UP
Confident onboarding

Guide sign-in, wallet setup, security and any required funding or permissions.

Measure: setup completion
04 / FIRST VALUE
Complete one job

Help the user finish the relevant first action: transfer, creator interaction or considered trade.

Measure: first value event
05 / RETURN
Build a habit

Offer a useful next step tied to the user's initial motivation and demonstrated behaviour.

Measure: repeat value event
Activation principle

Do not force every new user through the same feature tour. A person who arrived to send money should not be pushed into trading; a creator should not be interrupted with irrelevant market prompts. Let the first journey reflect the reason they came.

06 / RETENTION & GROWTH LOOPS

Give users a reason to come back — and a reason to invite others.

Utility loop

A successful transfer creates a reason to use the app again when another real payment need appears.

Creator loop

Creators publish; audiences discover and interact; meaningful engagement gives creators a reason to return.

Discovery loop

Users find relevant apps or assets; useful experiences create repeat exploration and sharing.

Referral loop

A user invites a recipient, collaborator or friend because the next action is more useful together.

These are proposed product-growth loops. Their existence and strength should be validated with cohort analysis; do not assume virality or organic growth simply because social or referral features exist.
07 / KPI FRAMEWORK

Measure the health of the growth engine.

Set baselines and targets with the product team after confirming event definitions, market availability and data quality. The metrics below are a measurement framework, not claimed Base performance.

ACQUISITION
Qualified acquisition cost

Spend ÷ new users who meet the defined qualified-entry criteria, by channel and segment.

ACQUISITION
Qualified visit rate

Visits from the intended audience ÷ total campaign landing visits.

ACTIVATION
First-value conversion

New eligible users completing their segment's first value event ÷ eligible new users.

ACTIVATION
Time to first value

Median time from first app session to the first successful meaningful action.

RETENTION
Meaningful repeat rate

Activated users completing another value event within a pre-defined cohort window.

QUALITY
Cost per retained user

Acquisition and activation cost ÷ users meeting the agreed retained-use definition.

MONETISATION
Net contribution per cohort

Attributed net revenue less incentives, variable costs and relevant servicing costs, where measurable.

QUALITY
Failed / reversed action rate

Share of attempts that fail, are reversed where applicable or require support intervention.

TRUST
Safety and support signals

Scam reports, mistaken approvals, complaints and support contacts per active cohort.

North-star metric proposal

Weekly active users completing at least one meaningful, successful product action — reported alongside repeat-use cohorts, transaction quality and safety guardrails. Define qualifying actions separately for transfers, creator activity and trading; never collapse them into one unqualified transaction count.

08 / 90-DAY ROADMAP

Prove one repeatable acquisition-to-value path.

DAYS 01–30 · DIAGNOSE

Choose the wedge.

  • Confirm priority launch markets and feature availability.
  • Interview new, activated and abandoned users in the chosen segment.
  • Audit onboarding, funnel analytics, attribution and support themes.
  • Select one first-value event and establish a baseline.
  • Build one segment-specific landing page and message framework.
DAYS 31–60 · PILOT

Run controlled tests.

  • Launch a small creator, partner or search-led acquisition pilot.
  • Compare use-case-led creative with product-led creative.
  • Test guided onboarding against the existing flow.
  • Review first-value conversion, failure reasons and cost by cohort.
  • Interview users who completed and abandoned the journey.
DAYS 61–90 · IMPROVE

Scale what earns its place.

  • Reallocate effort toward channels producing quality activations.
  • Improve the highest-friction onboarding step.
  • Introduce one behaviour-triggered lifecycle journey.
  • Measure repeat meaningful use and safety guardrails.
  • Present a scale / iterate / stop recommendation with evidence.
09 / RISKS & VALIDATION

Growth must not outrun trust.

Feature and market availability

Verify country, platform and feature eligibility before campaign promises or creator scripts go live.

Financial promotion and compliance

Review local rules, asset restrictions, risk disclosures, creator disclosures and paid media policies in every target market.

Speculation-led acquisition

Avoid messaging that implies guaranteed returns, easy profit or risk-free earning. Do not optimise solely for trading volume.

Incentive distortion and bots

Separate organic and incentivised cohorts, apply anti-sybil controls and report suspicious activity separately.

Wallet security and scams

Make signing, permissions, recovery and scam-prevention guidance visible at the moment it matters.

Measurement gaps

Agree event taxonomy, privacy boundaries, attribution windows and retention definitions before launch.
